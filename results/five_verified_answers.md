# Five Verified Answers

### 1. What was June churn?

**8.77% blended paid-subscription churn.**

This is based on 914 paid subscriptions ending in June out of 10,425 paid subscriptions active at the beginning of June.

For the monthly plan specifically, June churn was **11.17%**, compared with **3.71% in May**.

---

### 2. What drove the June churn increase?

**The March acquisition cohort was the primary driver.**

572 of the 914 June monthly subscription ends came from subscriptions that started in March, representing **62.6% of June monthly churn**.

The March cohort had approximately **32.24% June churn**, substantially higher than surrounding cohorts.

---

### 3. What happened in March?

**March had an abnormal acquisition spike associated with paid social.**

There were **3,423 March signups**, compared with 1,442 in February and 1,488 in April.

Approximately **77% of March signups came through paid social**, compared with roughly 20% in surrounding months.

---

### 4. What role did LAUNCH60 play?

**LAUNCH60 was a major contributor to June churn.**

There were **516 LAUNCH60 monthly subscriptions ending in June**, representing **56.5% of June monthly subscription ends**.

These subscriptions were priced at **₹160** and were predominantly created during the March campaign.

---

### 5. Should the monthly price be rolled back?

**No — not based on the June churn data.**

The June churn spike does not establish that the ₹399 → ₹449 price increase caused the increase. None of the monthly subscriptions that ended in June had a ₹449 starting price.

**Recommendation: keep ₹449 and evaluate the price using a clean post-June-1 renewal cohort before considering a rollback.**
